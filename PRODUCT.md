# Product

## Register

product

## Users
BreakGen serves makers, hardware founders, industrial designers, creative technologists, and ambitious enthusiasts who want to design programmable physical products without manually stitching together CAD, EDA, firmware, and fabrication tools. They usually have strong taste or strong product intent, but uneven confidence in manufacturing workflows. They are often deciding under time pressure, comparing options visually, and trying to move from concept to something buildable without losing authorship.

## Product Purpose
BreakGen is a creative and engineering environment for custom electronic products with printable physical shells. Its job is to turn human intent into structured, revisioned product state, then compile that state into previews, validation, mechanical outputs, electronics metadata, and export bundles. Success means the product feels expressive up front and trustworthy at the back: users can shape what they want, understand what the system is doing, and leave with artifacts they can actually act on.

## Brand Personality
Precise, cinematic, and uncompromising. BreakGen should feel like a serious product studio for physical computing, not a toy generator, not a generic AI dashboard, and not a beige enterprise tool. The emotional target is confident momentum: visitors should feel that the system understands both creative direction and engineering consequence.

## Anti-references
- Generic AI SaaS landing pages with pastel gradients, empty claims, and repetitive icon cards.
- Browser CAD tools that feel utilitarian but visually anonymous.
- Crypto-neon dark interfaces that confuse loudness with confidence.
- Dashboard-heavy marketing pages that explain the stack without showing product feel.
- Decorative glassmorphism used as a default instead of as a material decision.

## Design Principles
- Show the creative loop, not just the feature list: the strongest story is concept to build artifact in one continuous environment.
- Make technical trust visible: revisioning, validation, exports, and provenance should read as product advantages, not backend trivia.
- Keep the interface product-first: even on launch surfaces, the UI should feel like a tool with taste, not a marketing skin detached from the software.
- Use contrast with restraint: dark, hardware-native surfaces are correct, but hierarchy should come from composition, typography, and signal, not noise.
- Expand with discipline: the product can broaden beyond keyboards, but every new family must still feel like part of one coherent programmable-hardware system.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast for all core copy and controls. Motion should remain purposeful and respect reduced-motion preferences. The product should work for keyboard-first navigation, avoid relying on color alone for status, and keep technical language legible for both expert and aspirational users.
