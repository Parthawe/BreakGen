# Manufactured Products and Kickstarter Plan

BreakGen should use "coming soon" product families as market learning lanes, not as unverified manufacturing claims.

The practical wedge is a small programmable device that can be physically prototyped, photographed, costed, and shipped in limited quantity. A Kickstarter campaign should happen only after one focused hardware path clears prototype and pilot-build gates.

## Coming Soon Product Lanes

| Lane | Why it belongs | First manufacturable proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Split macro pads | Reuses MX footprint, plate, matrix, QMK, and enclosure assumptions. | 10 working units with switch plate, PCB, case, and firmware map. |
| Creator command consoles | Strong creator use case with stream, video, and productivity workflows. | USB-C deck with display/button grid and documented scene profiles. |
| MIDI sketch controllers | Musicians understand custom control surfaces and can test value quickly. | USB MIDI prototype with pads, encoders, sliders, and DAW mappings. |
| Accessibility input rigs | High-need user workflows where custom placement matters. | User-tested large-button or joystick rig with mounting notes. |
| Pedal and footswitch boards | Useful for guitar, synth, and live-performance control. | Rugged enclosure proof with stomp switches and expression input. |
| Dev-board enclosure kits | Good fabrication wedge even before BreakGen owns a full PCB. | Printed or laser-cut enclosure with screw, port, and board clearance notes. |

## Kickstarter Reality

Kickstarter's current hardware/product-design guidance requires working prototypes and does not allow a campaign to rely on photorealistic renderings. Creators are also expected to explain how the product will be produced and whether they have produced similar things before.

That means BreakGen should not launch a broad "custom hardware platform" campaign first. It should launch one narrow kit:

1. Pick one hero device.
2. Build and film the working prototype.
3. Build a 10-unit pilot run.
4. Lock the part list, supplier assumptions, packaging, and shipping model.
5. Open a pre-launch page and send prototype updates.
6. Launch with limited reward tiers and explicit production risks.

## Best First Campaign

Recommended first campaign:

> BreakGen Split Macro Kit: a small programmable control surface generated through BreakGen, shipped as a transparent prototype-backed kit.

Why this is the best first campaign:

- It is close to the existing compiler and footprint catalog.
- It can be useful with a simple PCB, plate, case, switches, and firmware.
- It demonstrates the BreakGen system without requiring every future family to be production-ready.
- It gives YC and backers a concrete object to judge.

## Campaign Page Structure

The page should answer five questions fast:

1. What is the device?
2. What does it do for a maker, streamer, musician, or founder?
3. What did BreakGen generate?
4. What has already been prototyped?
5. What are the manufacturing risks?

Suggested sections:

- Working prototype video
- Device photos
- BreakGen export evidence
- What is in the kit
- Build guide preview
- Timeline
- Risks and mitigations
- Why this becomes a bigger product

## Pre-Launch Metrics

Do not launch on hope. Launch after the signal is strong enough.

Minimum signal before campaign:

- 300 interested emails for the exact kit
- 30 user conversations
- 10 pilot units attempted
- 5 successful external builds or supervised build sessions
- supplier quotes for PCB, case, packaging, and fulfillment
- cost model with margin after shipping buffers

## Risk Rules

- Do not show renders that imply finished manufacturing quality.
- Do not promise multiple product families as campaign rewards.
- Do not offer too many variants.
- Do not set delivery dates from best-case supplier lead times.
- Do not hide firmware, assembly, or fulfillment risk.
